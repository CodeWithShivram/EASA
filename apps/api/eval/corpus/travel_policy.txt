Larkspur Analytics - Business Travel Policy

This policy covers booking, approval and conduct for business travel. Its purpose is to keep travellers safe and costs reasonable.

Approval

All international trips need written approval from a director before any booking is made. Domestic trips need line manager approval. Approval requests are submitted with travel request code TRV-330 in the finance system, together with the purpose of the trip and an estimated budget.

Booking

Flights, rail and hotels must be booked through the company travel agent, Bluefin Travel, so that the company knows where travellers are in an emergency. Bookings made elsewhere are not reimbursed unless the agent could not provide an option.

Economy class is the standard for all flights. Premium economy is allowed for flights longer than six hours. Business class requires approval from the chief financial officer and is only considered for flights over ten hours followed by a working day.

Rail travel is preferred over flying for journeys under four hours. Standard class is the norm; first class may be booked when it costs less than standard class.

Hotels

Hotel rooms should not exceed 180 euros per night in capital cities or 130 euros per night elsewhere, including taxes. Travellers who stay with friends or family instead of a hotel may claim a flat 40 euros per night.

Daily allowance

A per diem of 55 euros per day covers meals and incidental expenses on overnight trips. Travellers do not need receipts for the per diem. When meals are provided by a conference or client, the per diem is reduced by 15 euros per meal.

Safety

Travellers must register every trip with the security team at least five days before departure. Travel to countries rated high risk by the security team is not permitted without a specific risk assessment.

If something goes wrong while travelling, call the 24-hour assistance line printed on the back of the corporate card. The line can arrange medical help, replacement documents and emergency flights home.

Combining business and leisure

Employees may extend a business trip for personal leisure if the extension adds no cost to the company. Any extra nights, flights or insurance for the leisure part are paid by the employee.
